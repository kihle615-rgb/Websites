    Why we're different

    Four things we promise.

    1. 01

      Long-lasting luxury fragrances

      Staying power is a matter of concentration. An eau de parfum carries roughly 15–20% fragrance oil where an eau de toilette carries 5–15% — which is why one is still on your collar at six and the other has gone by lunchtime.

    2. 02

      Designer-inspired excellence

      Every bottle is built to an accord you already know. We tell you which one. You are paying for what is in the glass rather than for the name printed on it.

    The history

    Perfume is older
    than the alphabet.

    The oriental family — resin, amber, wood and spice — is named after the trade routes it came down. Here is the short version.

    1. c. 1200 BC

      The first chemist we can name

      A cuneiform tablet from Mesopotamia records a woman called Tapputi distilling flowers, oil and calamus into perfume. She is the earliest chemist known anywhere by name, and she was a perfumer.

    2. 9th century

      The first recipe book

      Al-Kindi, working in Baghdad, wrote the Book of the Chemistry of Perfume and Distillations — more than a hundred formulas for scented oils, salves and waters, and the equipment to make them.

    3. c. 1000 AD

      Rose, distilled

      Ibn Sina — Avicenna — refined steam distillation to pull the oil from rose petals without scorching them. Rose water has been made essentially his way for a thousand years since.

    4. 16th–17th century

      Attar down the trade routes

      Oil-based perfume — attar, worn a drop at a time rather than sprayed — moved between Arabia, Persia and Mughal India along the same roads as agarwood, frankincense and myrrh, long before alcohol perfumery reached Europe.

    5. Today

      Oud, and what it costs

      Oud is the dark resin an Aquilaria tree lays down when its heartwood is infected by a mould. Only a small share of wild trees ever make it, which is why the best grades trade for more by weight than gold — and why a good oud accord is worth knowing how to recognise.
